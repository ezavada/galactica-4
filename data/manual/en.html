Galactica-4

User’s Manual

Build an empire among the stars. Expand from your home system, develop its economy and technology, explore the galaxy and defend your colonies. Your opponents begin with their own home systems and pursue the same goal.

Starting a game

Choose a game title, the number of computer opponents, their skill, map size and star density. The computer advantage ranges from −3 to +3. Positive values help the computers; negative values help you. Each level changes their production benefits by 20%. Skill changes their decisions rather than your systems’ resources.

Map sizes range from 1×1 to 5×5 sectors. Density ranges from 1 to 10: higher density places more systems in the same area. Fast Game doubles movement and accelerates production. Fog of War limits enemy information to what your scanners can detect. These choices describe the galaxy you are about to create.

Choose Start, enter your player name and confirm. You begin with a home system and a colony hull nearly finished. Your home system has its own name, shown in the player comparison. Start with a small map and modest computer opposition while learning the controls. Open Game loads a saved galaxy instead of creating one. The manual is available before you start.

Reading and navigating the map

Select a star, ship, fleet or rendezvous point to show its information and available commands. An information window’s color suggests the owner; unknown or unowned subjects use neutral colors. When several objects overlap, the selection list lets you choose the intended one.

Use the wheel or zoom buttons to zoom. The view centers around the pointer when zooming with the wheel. Drag empty space, use the middle mouse button or hold the map-drag modifier to pan. The scrollbars also move the map. Fit shows the galaxy; Center follows your selected subject; Home returns to your home system. At full zoom out the background has no parallax. Decorative stars outside the playable area are not destinations.

Ownership, technology and population map displays help compare systems. Scanner circles, courses, grid lines, ship markers and labels can be shown or hidden independently. Hiding a drawing does not change the orders or information it represents.

Systems and production

Each system has its own population and technology. Population supports production; greater technology improves the ships you build and the strength of the system. Technology is local: a highly developed colony does not automatically upgrade every other colony.

Growth, Ships and Research share one allocation budget. The solid fill in a production slider shows the actual level. Its vertical marker and percentage show the target. Click or drag the bar to set that target. Changing one target redistributes the remaining budget among unlocked allocations. Lock an allocation to keep its target fixed; Equalize divides the available budget among the unlocked allocations.

The economy adjusts gradually toward the targets when turns advance. Setting Research high does not instantly replace the current production mix. Growth increases population, Ships pays for the selected hull and Research develops technology. A system’s production window provides the same controls as its information window.

New Colony Defaults chooses the initial allocations and hull for future colonies. Save applies the choices; Cancel leaves the saved defaults unchanged. A newly established colony asks you to choose its name and production priorities. Existing colonies retain their own choices.

The = button beside the sliders equalizes unlocked targets; = is also its keyboard shortcut while an owned system is selected or Production is open. The bare lock icons keep individual targets fixed. Send to shows the destination for newly built ships; Set chooses a map target and Clear removes it. Hull buttons switch production without a pause command.

The four ship types

TypeRoleLimitations
Defense SatelliteStationary protection for a system; economical local defense.Cannot travel to another system.
Scout/CourierFast exploration and transfer of technological knowledge between friendly systems.Unarmed and vulnerable in combat.
Colony ShipEstablishes a colony at an unowned system or occupies a conquered system.Slow and held in reserve during combat. Survives colonization and capture and can establish additional colonies.
BattleshipAttacks ships, protects fleets and defeats planetary resistance.More expensive; cannot establish a colony by itself.

A ship’s technology reflects the system that commissioned it. More advanced ships generally move faster and fight more effectively. Battleship classes advance with technology. Inspect the ship to see its class, damage, movement and destination information.

A standalone courier can acquire a higher technology library from a friendly system and deliver it to a less advanced friendly system. Other ships and fleets can deliver higher technology to a system, but battleships do not acquire a new library from it. A courier contained in a fleet does not independently refresh its library.

Building ships

Select an owned system and choose a hull. Production effort pays toward that hull over successive turns. The information window shows its progress and estimated time remaining. Completion at 100% commissions the ship; no extra payment is required. A hull under construction is not yet a ship on the map and cannot receive movement orders.

Switching hulls preserves the separate credit already paid toward each type. A newly selected hull’s cost is based on the system’s technology. Technology delivered by an arriving ship refreshes the current hull’s price without discarding its effort.

Default destinations can send newly completed mobile ships on their way automatically. Defense satellites stay at their system. The first ship of each role and newly achieved battleship classes receive a Stardock presentation. The Ships in system list shows commissioned ships and fleets available there.

Scrap marks a ship or fleet for recycling when the turn advances. Cancel Scrap reverses the mark before then. Scrapping recovers part of the build value; it is different from losing a ship in combat.

Movement and courses

Select an owned mobile ship or fleet, choose its movement command and then select a destination on the map. Cancel leaves the previous order intact. Orders describe movement for later turns; issuing one does not advance time. A destination may be a system, a rendezvous point or another eligible object.

Courses retain an ordered list of waypoints. Add appends a destination, Edit changes a selected waypoint, Delete removes it and Clear removes the course. Loop repeats the route when it reaches the end. Couriers can use a repeating route to circulate between colonies. The course window shows the current leg and estimates where available.

A moving object used as a waypoint is followed at its current location. Destroyed or unavailable targets may leave a coordinate destination instead. A ship does not complete several legs in one turn merely because they are close together. A ship inside a fleet follows the containing fleet’s orders rather than moving independently.

Patrol keeps a ship or fleet guarding its current location. Cancel patrol and give it orders to send it elsewhere. Course memory helps reuse orders while working with several ships. End Turn advances movement, resolves arrivals and combat, adjusts economies and refreshes scanner information.

Fleets and ship exchange

Combine compatible owned ships into a fleet so they move and fight together. Its speed is limited by its slowest member and its combat power comes from its ships. Fleets may contain groups inside other groups; those nested fleets retain their identities but follow the highest containing fleet while traveling.

The fleet window lists members and their condition. Use Exchange ships to move eligible ships or nested groups between containers at the same location. Transfers cannot put a group inside itself or one of its descendants. Splitting or extracting a group gives it independent orders again.

Fleet names, routes and detailed contents belong to their owner. Enemy information depends on detection. A fleet that loses some members remains a fleet while it has living members. Destruction of a top-level fleet has its own event; individual member losses are also counted in battle reports.

Left Arrow selects the ship or group’s containing fleet or system and opens its contents, remembering the child you came from. The remembered row stays visible even on a later page. In the exchange window, Right Arrow or Inspect opens the selected member; when entering a fleet, its first row is selected. The Parent fleet or Containing system button also moves up. At the top level, Left Arrow plays the “No more” cue and leaves the selection and turn unchanged.

Rendezvous and default destinations

Rendezvous points organize movement. Place one at a system or any chosen point in space, name it and use it as a destination. Its thicker marker pulses in the player’s color to help distinguish it from stars. It does not produce ships or establish ownership of a star.

A system’s default destination supplies orders to newly commissioned mobile ships. Select Set destination and choose the intended target. Clear removes the default. A rendezvous point can serve as an assembly location for ships from several colonies; give assembled fleets further orders after reviewing their members.

Moving or deleting a rendezvous point can affect orders that refer to it. Check the affected courses and destination information before ending the turn. A point anchored at a system and a free-space point are both usable destinations.

The first ship to reach an empty rendezvous point starts a new fleet and creates a notice asking its owner to name it. Tab or Next item selects the fleet and displays the notice; use Rename to give it a name. Observers can see a separate formation notice only when the fleet is detected. Further arrivals do not repeat the creation notice.

Scanners and fog of war

Scanners reveal nearby enemy objects. Detection can reveal progressively more information, from limited movement information through class and detailed statistics. A missing detail is not proof that a ship or system has no such property. Enemy commands remain unavailable even when you can inspect the subject.

Yellow-star names stay visible on the map with fog enabled. Undetected enemy home systems use substitute map names until they enter detection range. Compare Players deliberately shows the true homeworld names, so those names cannot simply be matched to an undiscovered map label.

Turning fog off at game creation shows all sides. Scanner-range drawing is a separate view option: hiding circles does not disable detection. Enemy event reports are accessible only when their subjects are currently detected, allied or visible in an all-visible game. Losing detection can hide those observer reports again.

Combat, invasion and repair

Ships arriving at an enemy ship or fleet fight its hostile forces. At an enemy star, space defenses are fought first. If attacking warships survive and clear the space defenders, they attack planetary resistance. Couriers and colony ships are held in reserve during combat, not a substitute for a battleship escort.

A system can lose its last resistance and become unowned. A surviving colony ship can then occupy it; without that ship, defeating resistance does not establish your colony. Ground combat can kill population even when an invasion fails. A battleship’s strength, technology and existing damage matter, so compare forces rather than relying only on ship counts.

Battle reports show each participant’s own casualties and the opposing losses. Defeated resistance, captured systems and destroyed ships or fleets have separate reports. A surviving system can report defending-ship losses after wiping out the attackers. Reports retain the names and location from the battle, even if the subject is later renamed or destroyed.

Damaged mobile ships repair at friendly systems when they are docked and neither patrolling nor moving. Fleets repair their eligible member ships there. The system’s technology supplies the repair budget; satellites do not receive that mobile-ship repair. Inspect damage and health before assigning the next mission.

When a docked ship is completely repaired, its system’s human owner receives a private repair notice. A fleet receives one notice only when all its ships are repaired. Find it in Events or the attention list; View report reopens the message.

Events and attention

Events lists reports from the last completed turn. Select a row to review its full text and recorded location. Go to centers the subject or surviving location. View report replays supported battle and outcome presentations. Reviewing or acknowledging a report does not advance the turn.

Next item and Tab visit subjects asking for attention: completed ships, idle mobile units, finished courses, colonies and important private messages. Manual selection or review can satisfy an associated request. End Turn asks for confirmation if attention requests or presentations remain unreviewed; Review lets you examine them before continuing.

Show enemy events controls automatic playback of accessible reports about other players. Turning it off leaves those reports available for manual review in Events. Private reports never become public merely because fog is disabled. Quiet autoplay avoids interrupting each turn with automatic presentations.

Continue or Escape acknowledges a message. Previous and Next page longer lists or narratives. Destruction feedback plays at the battle location; reduced motion suppresses the visual effect. Victory, defeat and a game with no surviving players have retained conclusion reports that can be reviewed without taking another turn.

Colonizing an uninhabited system automatically shows a private success report. Continuing selects the new system and displays its naming and production guidance. Other players receive a different report only when they can detect or otherwise know the system; Show enemy events controls automatic observer playback, while Events keeps manual replay available.

If there are no remaining attention items, Tab or Next item plays a short “No more” cue without changing the selection or advancing the turn. The Sound setting also controls this cue.

The first construction of a hull type or a higher battleship class is announced to its owner and other players. The owner receives the illustrated Stardock presentation. Eligible observers receive a report naming the faction, class, technology and construction system; the artwork remains private. Observer reports follow detection and Show enemy events, and remain available for manual replay. Reports remember the construction details even after the ship departs or names change.

A new drive-class discovery is announced to its owner and other players. Only the owner sees the illustrated Lab. Observers receive a report naming the discovering system, faction and technology, subject to detection and Show enemy events. Events permits manual replay and preserves the original discovery details.

Comparisons and lists

Compare Players is a game-menu command rather than a star command. It shows the empires’ comparative information and true homeworld names. Your Star Systems compares your colonies’ technology, population, production, construction and defenses. Select column headings to sort the system table and select a row to visit the colony.

The fleet and ship roster provides a larger view of your mobile forces. Sorting, paging and selecting a row help find damaged units, destinations and groups needing orders. Double-click or use the displayed navigation command to go to a listed subject. Lists retain their ordering and window geometry preferences.

Visible rows are only part of a paged list. Use Previous and Next when the list extends beyond its current page. Ownership and visibility determine which commands and enemy details are available; opening a comparison does not grant control of another player’s pieces.

Saving and open games

File → Save writes the current game. Save As chooses a different name and asks before replacing an existing desktop file. Browser saves use an available file picker or download a game document. Keep the downloaded file somewhere you can find when reopening the game.

Open loads a saved galaxy with its ships, colonies, orders, production, reports and current turn. Revert asks before discarding changes and returns to the last saved state. A failed load leaves the currently open game intact. Galactica-4 game documents use the .gad4.json extension; legacy Galactica v3 saves are not compatible.

New Game can create another galaxy while keeping the current one open. Open Games lets you switch among the games in this session. Returning to a game restores its own selection, map view and orders. Save each game’s progress before closing the application or browser tab.

Settings and view controls

Settings selects the interface language. The choices always use each language’s native name and script. Language changes update the game’s labels and number formatting immediately. When settings do not fit the window, use their page controls or left and right arrow keys.

Map display selects ownership, technology or population coloring. View controls navigation and drawing options. Settings also provides hover help and enemy-event playback. Lock icons beside production sliders protect their target allocations. In a browser, reduced motion follows the system or browser preference. Full screen expands the game display where supported; exit full screen through the same command or your browser.

Preferences remembered for new games and choices stored in a saved game can differ. A restored game keeps its own drawing and enemy-event choices. The manual opens separately, leaving the current game and turn intact. If it cannot open, acknowledge the message, allow new tabs or choose a default browser and try again.

Sound in Settings controls event and attention cues for every open game. It is remembered separately from saved games. Turning it off stops current playback; turning it on plays a short confirmation cue. Hidden reports and quiet autoplay do not play event sounds.

You can also open Settings from the start screen, before creating a game. New game defaults remembers map size, density, AI skill and advantage, fast game and fog of war. Select numeric values directly; Save accepts the draft and Cancel discards it. These defaults apply to future setups and fresh launches. The setup already on screen, retained games and the fixed tutorial keep their own choices. Escape closes Settings. Drag its title to move it; its position is remembered and stays within the window.

New colony defaults is available in Settings, including at startup, and directly from the game menu. Save remembers the hull and growth, shipbuilding and research allocations for future colonies and games; Cancel leaves them unchanged. Existing systems keep their allocations. As in the original game, opening a saved game applies your application colony defaults to the local human player. Computer players keep their own defaults. Fixed tutorial practice neither reads nor writes these application preferences.

Full screen is also available in Settings at startup and during a game. Its highlight reflects the actual display mode. Exiting fullscreen outside the game updates the setting. If a request fails, Settings shows a message, keeps the saved choice and lets you retry; the window remains modal.

The introduction appears when Galactica starts. Use the arrow buttons or left/right arrow keys to read it at your own pace; manual paging stops automatic scrolling. Close or Escape returns to setup. Settings lets you turn the startup introduction off and open About independently. About shows the original artwork and credits without changing your game. The triangle button or T starts the tutorial; Close tutorial restores your previous game.

On a new installation, closing the introduction starts the tutorial automatically. Close tutorial returns to setup. Existing preferences and later launches skip automatic onboarding. If the tutorial cannot start, Galactica tries to open the manual. When preference storage is unavailable, you can still start the tutorial yourself.

About identifies the application’s development build by its commit. “Modified” means the checkout had changes when launched or packaged. Include this identifier when reporting a problem. If build metadata is unavailable, About says so; the PDG runtime version is separate.

Strategy

Expand early. A colony ship can occupy a promising neutral star but cannot fight its way past an enemy defense. Scout routes expose useful systems before you risk slower colony ships. Nearby colonies support each other more readily than isolated systems far beyond your fleet’s reach.

Balance investment and immediate needs. Growth builds future productive capacity, Research improves what a system can build and Ships supplies the forces you need now. Because actual allocations approach targets gradually, plan changes several turns ahead. Pausing a hull and changing allocations solve different problems.

Use couriers to spread technology from strong research colonies. Remember the restriction on couriers inside fleets. Establish rendezvous routes to gather forces, inspect the slowest fleet member and leave adequate defense behind. A large fleet can still be strategically late if its destination is far away.

Before invading, plan both the battle and occupation. Battleships clear resistance; colony ships establish ownership. Damage and reserves change an encounter’s outcome, and an undefended route can expose your transports. Repair and regroup after difficult battles. Read events, review idle units and use comparisons to identify colonies or fleets falling behind.

Keyboard reference

Names are edited directly over their map labels in a white text field. The original name is selected initially; typing replaces it. Click in the field or use arrow keys to move the caret. Enter accepts the name and Escape cancels the draft. Set/Clear destination controls are beside the aligned Send to label; Clear appears only when a destination exists. Ships in system and Exchange ships appear last in the radial controls only when there are completed ships or fleets to inspect.

Buttons display their relevant shortcuts. The following common commands work while the map is available; dialogs and report windows consume their own keys.

Network play and tutorial

Galactica-4 currently supports local games against computer opponents. Hosting, joining network games and the interactive tutorial are not yet available in this version. The original v3 manual’s host, join, password and server instructions therefore do not apply to the current game. Local saved games and multiple open games do not require a server.
